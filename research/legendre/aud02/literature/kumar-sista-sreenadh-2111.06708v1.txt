Abstract
In this paper, we study an initial-boundary value problem of Kirchhoﬀ type
involving memory term for non-homogeneous materials ( Dα). The purpose of
this research is threefold. First, we prove the existence an d uniqueness of weak
solutions to the problem ( Dα) using the Galerkin method. Second, to obtain
numerical solutions of (Dα) eﬃciently, we develop a L1 type [1] backward Euler-
Galerkin FEM, which is O(h + k2−α) accurate, where α (0 < α < 1) is the
order of fractional time derivative, h and k are the discretization parameters
for space and time directions, respectively. Next, to achie ve the optimal rate
of convergence in time, we propose a fractional Crank-Nicol son-Galerkin FEM
based on L2-1 σ scheme [2]. We prove that the numerical solutions of this
scheme converge to the solution of ( Dα) with accuracy O(h + k2). We also
derive a priori bounds on numerical solutions for the propos ed schemes. Finally,
some numerical experiments are conducted to validate our th eoretical claims.
Keywords: Nonlocal, Finite element method (FEM), Fractional time der ivatives,
Fractional Crank-Nicolson scheme, Integro-diﬀerential e quations.

There are various notions of fractional derivatives other t han the Caputo derivative,
which include Riemann-Liouville, Gr¨ unwald-Letnikov, Weyl, Marchaud, and Riesz
fractional derivatives [3, 8]. Among these, Caputo fractio nal derivative and Riemann-
Liouville fractional derivative are the most commonly foun d in literature [3]. These
two fractional derivatives are related to each other by the f ollowing relation, see [3]
CDαtu(t) = RDαt (u(t) −u(0)), (1.2)
where RDαtu is the Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative deﬁned by
RDαtu(t) := 1Γ(1 −α) ddt∫ t0 1(t −s)αu(s)ds. (1.3)
It is worth noting that the fractional derivatives other tha n Caputo fractional deriva-
tive require initial condition containing the limiting val ue of fractional derivatives at
t = 0 [3], which has no physical interpretation. An advantage o f choosing Caputo
fractional derivative in the problem ( Dα) is that it allows the initial and boundary
conditions in the same way as those for integer order diﬀeren tial equations.
There are many physical and biological processes in which th e mean-squared dis-
placement of the particle motion grows only sublinearly wit h time t, instead of linear

growth. For instance, acoustic wave propagation in viscoel astic materials [6], cancer
invasion system [5], anomalous diﬀusion transport [4], whi ch cannot be described
accurately by classical models having integer order deriva tives. Therefore the study
of fractional diﬀerential equations has evolved immensely in recent years.
Problems involving fractional time derivatives have been studied by many researchers,
for instance, see [7, 10, 11]. Analytical solutions of fract ional diﬀerential equations
are expressed in terms of Fox H-functions, Green functions, and hypergeometric
functions. Such special functions are more complex to compu te, which restricts the
applications of fractional calculus in applied sciences. T his motivates the researchers
to develop numerical algorithms for solving fractional diﬀ erential equations.
There are two predominant discretization techniques in tim e for fractional diﬀerential
equations, namely Gr¨ unwald-Letnikov approximation [12]and L1 type approximation
[2, 13]. The second category, namely the L1 type approximati on scheme, is based on
piecewise interpolation of the integrand in deﬁnition (1.1 ) of the Caputo fractional
derivative. Lin and Xu [13] developed the L1 scheme based on p iecewise linear
interpolation for Caputo fractional derivative and Legend re spectral method in space
for the following time fractional PDE in one space dimension

to C 2 ([0,T ];H 2(Ω) ∩H 10 (Ω)). Recently, Alikhanov [2] proposed a modiﬁcation of
the L1 type scheme in time direction and diﬀerence scheme in s pace direction for the
problem (1.4). In his work, the author proved that the conver gence rate is O(h2 +k2)
for the solutions belonging to C 3 ([0,T ];C 4(Ω)).

The models discussed above behave accurately only for a perf ectly homogeneous
medium, but in real-life situations, a large number of heter ogeneities are present,
which causes some memory eﬀect or feedback term. These pheno mena cannot be
described by classical PDEs, which motivates us to study tim e fractional PDEs for
non-homogeneous materials. We note that this class of equat ions has not been ana-
lyzed in the literature yet, and this is the ﬁrst attempt to es tablish new results for
the model (Dα).
The salient feature of our problem is its doubly nonlocal nat ure due to the presence
of Kirchhoﬀ term and fractional derivatives or memory term. The memory term
incorporates the history of the phenomena under investigat ion, which is crucial in
evolutionary processes. This structure induces additiona l diﬃculties in the use of
classical methods of PDEs. In the ﬁrst part of this article, w e show the existence and
uniqueness of weak solutions for the problem ( Dα) by applying the Galerkin method.
Further, we discretize the domain in space direction by usin g a conforming FEM [21],
keeping the time variable continuous. We deﬁne a new Ritz-Vo lterra type operator
as an intermediate function and derive error estimates of O(h) for semi-discrete so-
lutions of the problem ( Dα).
Finally, we ﬁnd numerical solutions of ( Dα) by discretizing the domain in the time
direction also. Qiao et al. [24] studied ( Dα) having weakly singular kernel and with-
out Kirchhoﬀ term and derived O(h2 +k2−α) convergence rate by using diﬀerence
schemes in space and L1 type scheme in the time direction. For the case b = 0 in
(Dα), Manimaran et al. [33] used L1 type methods for fractional d erivative and New-
ton methods for nonlinearity to obtain O(h2 +k2−α) accuracy for suﬃciently smooth
solutions.
The novelty of our work is to obtain the optimal rate of conver gence O(k2) in time
for the numerical solutions of the problem ( Dα). For nonlinear PDEs, linearization
techniques have the advantage over Newton’s iterations met hod in terms of compu-
tational cost and storage. This is the motivation for us to de velop a linearized fully
discrete formulation based on the Galerkin FEM in space and t he L1 type scheme in
the time direction. This scheme is proved to be accurate of O(h +k2−α). Further,
to increase the accuracy in time, we propose a new linearized numerical algorithm
based on L2-1 σ scheme. In this scheme, we prove that numerical solutions co nverge

the discretization of memory term demands large computer st orage. To resolve these

8 Conclusions
In this work, we have established the well-posedness of weak formulation for time
fractional integro-diﬀerential equations of Kirchhoﬀ typ e for non-homogeneous ma-
terials. We have derived error estimates for semi-discrete formulation by discretizing
the domain in space direction using a conforming FEM and obta ined convergence
rate O(h) in the energy norm. Further, to obtain the numerical soluti on for this
class of equations, we have developed and analyzed two diﬀer ent kinds of eﬃcient
numerical schemes. First, we derived that convergence rate is (2-α) in the time di-
rection, which is better than ﬁrst order but not optimal. Nex t, to obtain the optimal
rate of convergence, we proposed a new Fractional Crank-Nic olson-Galerkin FEM
based on the L2-1 σ scheme and proved that numerical solutions converge with th e
O(h +k2) rate of accuracy. Finally, numerical experiments reveal t hat theoretical
error estimates are sharp.
