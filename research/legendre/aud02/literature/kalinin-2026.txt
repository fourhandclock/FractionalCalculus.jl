1 Iteratedintegralinequalitiesandresultingﬁxedpoints
The integral inequalityfor functions of one variable introduced in the classical work [12]
of Gronwall, known nowadays as Gronwall’s inequality, is a proven method to analyse
the behaviour of solutions to ordinary and stochastic diﬀerential equations. This crucial
inequality provides the foundation for a variety of existence and uniqueness results as
wellascomparison,growthandstabilityestimatesthatarefundamentaltotherespective
analyticalframeworks.
Standard textbooks on ordinary diﬀerential equations that emphasise this fact include
[7,C h a p .1 ] ,[13,C h a p .3 ]a n d[2, Chaps. 2 and 4]. Relevant references on stochastic dif-
ferential equations are, for instance, [19,C h a p .4 ] ,[25, Sect. 5.2], [33,C h a p .9 ]a n d[29,
Chaps.2and5].Givenitsinherentversatility,Gronwall’sinequalityisfrequentlyemployed
in this literature. Reﬁned versions can be found in [31, Sect. 1.3], [28, p. 128], [33, p. 543]
and[29,p.45].
Initially, Gronwall’s inequality was extended in various directions by Bellman [5],
Jones [20], Willett [37], Schmaedeke and Sell [34], Herod [18], Helton [16], Wright, Klasi
and Kennebeck [38], Pachpatte [30] and Groh [11]. For further early articles, see the ref-
erences therein. Versions of Gronwall’s inequality for fractional kernels that may admit
singularities were inductively derived by Henry [17, pp. 188–189]. More recently, these
andothertypesofpotentiallysingularkernelswereinvestigatedin[ 1,9,26,27,36,39,40].
As shown in Examples1.8, 2.10 and 2.11, all these integral inequalities involving kernels
arecoveredbytheresolventinequalityonpreorderedsetsinCorollary 1.5.
